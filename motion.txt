Read CLAUDE.md first.

Motion and haptics. The redesign is in. This pass makes the app feel like something
rather than a form.

The rule that governs everything here: **every animation and every haptic must
correspond to a state change the user caused.** Nothing fires on scroll, on render, on
mount of a screen the user simply navigated to, or on data arriving. Feedback that
happens for no reason is worse than no feedback — it reads as a glitch.

Build the systems first, then apply them. Do not scatter one-off Haptics calls or
inline Animated values across screens.

============================================================
PART 1 — A haptic vocabulary, defined once
============================================================

Create `src/lib/haptics.ts` as the only place expo-haptics is imported. Everything else
calls named intents, never raw styles. A named vocabulary is what stops this becoming
a phone that buzzes constantly.

  tap()        — Selection. Chips, segmented controls, tier cards, tab switches.
  press()      — Impact Light. Any button press.
  complete()   — Impact Medium. A task completes, by swipe, timer, or check.
  triumph()    — Notification Success. Day sealed, squad joined, badge earned. Rare.
  refuse()     — Notification Warning. An action the app declines — start day 1 with
                 no tier, ping with none left, out-of-range input.
  fail()       — Notification Error. A write that failed. Rare, and honest.

Rules:
- One haptic per gesture. If a swipe completes a task **and** seals the day, fire
  `triumph()` only — never two in sequence.
- A Settings toggle disables all of it. Persist it. Some people hate this.
- Never on a repeating timer, never on a list re-render.

============================================================
PART 2 — Motion primitives
============================================================

Reanimated, on the UI thread. No JS-driven opacity or layout animation.

Define a shared timing vocabulary in the theme — durations and easings named by intent,
not by number, so the app moves consistently:

  instant  ~120ms  — press feedback
  quick    ~220ms  — most state changes
  settle   ~380ms  — things arriving or leaving
  moment   ~700ms  — celebration beats only

**Respect Reduce Motion.** Read `AccessibilityInfo.isReduceMotionEnabled` once at app
start and subscribe to changes. When on, every animation below becomes an instant state
change — not a slower one, an instant one. Haptics stay.

============================================================
PART 3 — Apply it
============================================================

**Buttons** — press scales to ~0.97 over `instant` and releases with a small spring.
`press()` on press-in, not on release. That timing is what makes a button feel
physical.

**Task completion** — the highest-value moment in the app, and it happens six times a
day. On complete: `complete()`, the checkbox fills from the centre, the label strikes
through over `quick`, the row settles to textMid, and **the SegmentBar's next segment
fills** with a spring. The segment filling is the part that makes progress feel real —
get it right.

**Swipe deck** — already has gesture handling; keep it. Add: the card's exit carries
into the next card rising slightly from beneath rather than appearing. `complete()` on
a right-swipe commit, `tap()` on a left-swipe defer.

**The Wall** — cells fade and scale in on a stagger when Track first mounts, ~8ms
apart, left to right. Once only per app session, not on every tab visit. Today's cell
holds a slow, low-amplitude pulse on its outline — it should read as alive, not as
blinking.

**Day number** — when the day advances, the number counts up rather than cutting.
`settle`. Once, on genuine day change.

**Timer** — the ring drains continuously and smoothly, driven from wall-clock time, not
from a per-frame counter. At T-10 seconds the numerals shift to accent. On completion:
`triumph()`, the ring completes, and the screen holds for a `moment` before returning.

**Screen transitions** — tabs switch instantly, no animation; animating tab content is
the fastest way to make an app feel slow. Pushed screens (timer, My Challenge,
Settings) slide from the right over `settle`. Modal sheets (ping, dev, capture) rise
with a spring and dim the ground behind them.

**Day complete** — the one place to spend real motion. `triumph()`, the ground shifts
to the celebration treatment, the day number scales briefly, and the seal message
arrives over a `moment`. This fires once a day at most. Make it worth waiting for.

**Empty states** — fade in over `quick` rather than appearing. An empty list that
snaps into existence reads as an error.

**Pull to refresh** on Squad and Track: `tap()` at the threshold, not on release.

============================================================
CONSTRAINTS
============================================================

Do not change data flow, service calls, or the snapshot model. Do not add an animation
library — Reanimated and expo-haptics are already here. Do not animate anything on
first paint of the app; a cold launch should show content, not choreography.

Performance: nothing here may drop the swipe deck below 60fps on device. If an
animation competes with the gesture, the gesture wins.

After: npx tsc --noEmit, npx expo lint, and a short report — the haptic vocabulary as
built, every place motion was added, what Reduce Motion turns off, and anything you
could not verify without a device. Haptics cannot be tested off-device at all; say so
plainly rather than implying they work.
